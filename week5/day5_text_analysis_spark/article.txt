
Maria Prymachenko was a Ukrainian folk artist known for imaginative paintings, bright colours, and fantastic creatures. 
She was born in the village of Bolotnia near Kyiv in 1909. Her family lived close to Ukrainian traditions, embroidery, craft, and folk stories.
After childhood illness affected her mobility, she began drawing and developed a remarkable artistic imagination.
Prymachenko created paintings with mythical birds, unusual animals, flowering trees, and colourful patterns.
She worked with gouache and watercolour. Her art combined village traditions with personal symbols and stories.
Although she was often connected with naive art, her style was original and powerful.
Her paintings explored nature, peace, hope, and the danger of war. Works about nuclear war and destruction expressed her opposition to violence.
Her art gained international recognition, including exhibitions in Kyiv and Paris.
She received important cultural awards.
Today, Maria Prymachenko represents Ukrainian identity, creativity, and resilience. Her works are displayed in museums and inspire artists, educators, and cultural activists.
Despite the loss of some paintings during the war in Ukraine, her artistic legacy continues to encourage people around the world.
